EYES DOWN

New York, 1985. Your apartment on the 31st floor, after 11 PM, in the rain. Things made of black slime come in while you do your evening routine. Keep living normally. If you look one in the eye, it knows you can see it, and it hunts you.

Its own page at /eyes, for a headset or a screen · shares no code with THE MARKET · the creatures' mind is Claude Haiku 5.5 through our worker

The apartment

A one-bedroom studio, 7 × 5 m, 2.6 m ceiling. One window wall faces the skyline. Each numbered spot is somewhere a chore takes you, and somewhere a creature can be waiting.

A night

11:00 PM to 6:00 AM in about 15 minutes of play. The night's chores are on a note on the fridge, and the order is up to you. Each chore takes you somewhere and makes you look somewhere, so the creatures wait where you will have to look.

The chores (night 1)

  1. Chain the front door
  2. Heat dinner on the stove
  3. Eat at the table
  4. Watch the late news on the CRT
  5. Answer the wall phone (a voice that knows your name)
  6. Wash the dishes
  7. Brush your teeth at the mirror
  8. Lights out, get into bed

Sleep (the last chore)

You lie in bed with your eyes shut (hold a button) until 6 AM. You can't see anything, only hear them: breathing at the bedside, the mattress sinking, your name whispered. Opening your eyes is allowed, but whatever is at the bed is looking at you.

The creatures

Black, wet, glossy slime that drips and merges as it moves, with pale eyes. Six body kinds: a tall stander, a crawler for floor or ceiling, a pool, a clinger on walls, a small sitter on furniture, and a hanger from above. One on night 1, six by night 5.

Being seen

Eye contact is the one hard rule. Everything else adds to a creature's suspicion. You can't see the numbers, but you feel them: a heartbeat in the controllers, ringing in your ears, the lights browning out.

Eye contact, step by step

Your gaze is the centre of your view (the headset's direction). If it rests on a creature's eyes for 0.3 s, it notices: its eyes widen and you hear a wet click. Look away within the next 0.5 s and you get away with it, but its suspicion jumps. Hold the look and it hunts. Eye contact in the mirror counts too.

What calms them

Doing chores at a normal pace, looking through them as if they're furniture, sitting through the news, picking things up as usual. Suspicion drains slowly while you act normal.

Their tests (Haiku)

Every few seconds the mind sees what you did and orders a test: sit beside you, knock a mug off the counter, whisper your name, ring the phone with no one there, turn the TV channel, step into your path. Whether you react is what it learns.

Lose, win, progress

Lose

  • Hunted: the lights die and the creature comes for you. You have about 4 seconds to get behind the bathroom door or under the covers and shut your eyes. Then it searches. If it finds you, you die and the night is over.
  • Too many creatures fully suspicious at once: they all stop pretending. Death.
  • At 6 AM with chores undone: your routine broke and they know. The night fails.

Win

  • A night: every chore done, in bed, eyes shut until 6 AM. Grey dawn comes through the rain and they slide back into the vents.
  • The game: five nights, Monday to Friday. On Friday at dawn one stays on the end of the bed, and you have to get up and leave for work without looking at it.

Progress

  • Each night adds a creature, a chore and new tests: the power cuts out, a neighbour knocks, a creature mimics the voice on the phone.
  • A grade for each night: composure (how little you gave away).
  • Saved per login on the server (the night you reached and your best grades), so you start from the night you reached.
  • Morning paper: at each dawn you read the mind's notes on you ("He paused at the mirror. I think he knows.").

Making it feel real

The window

A layered skyline at true distances: brick and art-deco towers with lit windows and blinds, blinking aircraft lights, water towers, neon signs, streams of headlights 31 floors down, and low overcast lit orange by sodium streetlights from below. Rain on the glass bends the view, with streaks and drops. Distant lightning.

Light and lens

Real-scale lighting: a tungsten lamp, the CRT's flickering glow lighting the room, fridge light, neon through the blinds with moving shadows, headlights sweeping across the ceiling. Depth of field that focuses where you look, bloom, film grain and slight chromatic fringe on screen. Shadows from the main lamp and the window.

Materials and physics

CC0 photo-scanned textures and models from Poly Haven: parquet, plaster, wood paneling, tiles, a 1980s CRT, microwave, stove, leather sofa, boombox. Mugs, cans, pillows, books and the pot are physics objects (Rapier), so you can grab, drop and knock them.

Sound

3D positional (HRTF): rain on the glass louder near the window, fridge hum, the radiator knocking, a neighbour's TV through the wall, sirens, the CRT's whine, wet movement behind you. Heartbeat rumble through the controllers.

The CRT

The TV is a curved glass tube with scanlines, phosphor glow and static between channels. It shows the late news, test patterns and late movies, and sometimes something that shouldn't be on.

Cost

About 12 MB of pictures and models, downloaded once. In the headset there's no screen blur or glow, and two lights cast shadows. On a screen everything is on. One Haiku call every 5 seconds while you play (capped per day, like DEAD END).
