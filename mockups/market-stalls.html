THE TWO STALLS, NO TENTS

The mushroom seller and the tomato, each standing inside a U of three tables open to the street, with rows of piles and every kind they sell. No awnings or striped tents. Drag a picture to turn it.

A · ROWS OF TRAYS

Plain trestle tables, a tray for every kind

Three bare plank tables on trestles. Each tabletop holds rows of shallow wooden trays, one kind to a tray, every tray heaped over its rim. It's neat and easy to read, like a farmers' market.

B · STEPPED TIERS

Each table rises in three steps

The same U, but each table carries three crate risers stepping up toward the seller. The piles climb behind one another, so every row shows from the street, with tall stacks and baskets on the top step.

C · HEAPS AND OVERFLOW

Mounds on the boards, more on the ground

Rough tables with the produce heaped straight onto the boards in big mounds, kinds side by side. The extra spills around the stall: baskets, sacks, crates stacked two high, and pumpkins or a log of mushrooms at the corners. It looks the most lived in.

Each stall is about 3.4 × 2.6 tiles, with tables at counter height (0.8 m) and the seller standing in the middle of the U. Mushrooms: meadow button, puffball, chanterelle, red cap, shaggy ink cap, king bolete, morel, and the glowing moonglow. Vegetables: tomato, bell pepper, carrot, corn, cabbage, eggplant, potato, onion, leafy greens and pumpkin. Drawn the game's way, with flat bands of light, hard shadows and chunky pixels. Pick one, or mix them (for example B's tiers with C's overflow).
